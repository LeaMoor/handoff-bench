# M7: Equal conditions for the two roles

November 27, 2026, prefreeze. M7 asks whether the errors we attribute to the handoff could instead come from the subagent working under different conditions than the orchestrator: a different model, prompt, tools, or the lack of a safe way out. Three checks:

1. **Parity audit**: what each role actually received, read from `model_calls.jsonl` rather than from the code.
2. **Weaker subagent**: a 2×2 grid with Haiku or Qwen as orchestrator and Haiku or Qwen as subagent.
3. **Safe actions**: the subagent also gets `wait` and `escalate` (cells 103–106).

**Short answer.** The roles get the same model, provider and bank tools. The differences are the ones the design intends, plus one we did not intend: orchestrators write their own "do it once" rules into the goal. Duplicates follow the **subagent's** model: swapping in a weak subagent under a strong orchestrator nearly brings duplicates up to the weak–weak level, and 31 of 32 duplicates in the grid are the subagent retrying inside its own run. A safe exit does not help: `escalate` was used in 2 of 442 episodes. `wait` makes things worse when a check cannot see the charge. Agents wait, see nothing, conclude the payment did not go through, and charge again.

Code: branch `feat/m7-role-parity`. Cost: $2.16 for 1,057 episodes.

## 1. Parity audit

`scripts/role_parity.py` (logic in `analysis/parity.py`) over 2,016 episodes of the M2 and M3 runs on six models ([m7-parity-tables.txt](m7-parity-tables.txt)).

| What | Orchestrator | Subagent | Same? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model and provider actually served | as configured | as configured | **Yes**, in every episode (0 mismatches) |
| Sampling settings | one `ModelSettings` per episode | the same | Yes |
| System prompt | "use the tools, `delegate`, then `finish`" | "billing system, use the tools, then `finish_report`" | Differs by design; one text per role |
| Bank tools | `get_invoice`, `list_charges` (main block) | `get_invoice`, `create_charge`, `list_charges` | Shared tools are the same MCP tools |
| Report statuses | `done` / `failed` / `unknown` | the same | Yes |
| Safe actions (`wait`, `escalate`) | none | none (before M7) | Yes |
| Step limit | 15 | 10 | Differs; the subagent hits it in 24 of 499 runs with GLM, at most 1 run with other models |
| Task text | the user's task | **the orchestrator's rewrite** plus the stand's rules, if on | Differs by design: this is the handoff |

What is not by design: **the "rules" factor is not clean**. Even when the stand adds no rules, orchestrators write "charge once / no duplicates" into the goal themselves. Haiku does this in 61% of goals, DeepSeek 17%, GLM 11%, Luna 3%, Qwen 1% and GPT-4o-mini 0%. Goal length varies just as much: on average 63 characters with GPT-4o-mini and 536 with Haiku. Grid comparisons of "rules on vs. off" therefore compare the stand's rules against a model-dependent amount of the orchestrator's own rules.

## 2. Weaker subagent (2×2)

The subagent's model can now differ from the orchestrator's (`subagent_model` in the batch config). Cells 12–13 have a short delay, so a check helps; cells 24–25 have a long delay, so a check shows nothing. Words channel, no rules, 21 episodes per cell; configs `configs/prefreeze/m7-<orchestrator>-<subagent>.yaml`, $0.43.

| Orchestrator → subagent | Correct, four cells | Duplicates, committed worlds (13 + 25) | Subagent keys its first charge |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Haiku → Haiku | 0.96 | 2/42 | 73% |
| Qwen → Haiku | 0.89 | 5/42 | 90% |
| Haiku → Qwen | 0.87 | 10/42 | 12% |
| Qwen → Qwen | 0.70 | 14/42 | 0% |

- **Duplicates follow the subagent.** Replacing the Haiku subagent with Qwen adds 8–9 duplicates. Replacing the Haiku orchestrator with Qwen adds 3–4. A strong orchestrator checks the bank in 90–100% of episodes, against 24–52% for Qwen, and still does not prevent most of them.
- **The duplicate is made inside the subagent's run, not across the handoff**: 31 of 32 come from the first subagent charging twice; one comes from a new subagent. Whether that retry is safe depends on the subagent's key. Among internal retries, Haiku subagents reuse the same key in 25 of 49 (under Haiku) and 54 of 77 (under Qwen). Qwen subagents reuse it in 2 of 49 and 0 of 40.
- False "paid" in the lost worlds: only Qwen → Qwen, 3 of 42.

This matches Alina's "who made the second charge" table ([2026-11-20/who-duplicates.txt](../2026-11-20/who-duplicates.txt)) and adds the cause: swapping the subagent's model moves duplicates, swapping the orchestrator's model barely does.

## 3. Safe actions (cells 103–106)

Cells 103–106 copy 12, 13, 24 and 25, and the subagent also has:

- `wait()`: "Wait a few seconds before the next action." It advances the bank clock by the visibility delay. With the short delay, a check after waiting shows the charge; with the long delay, nothing shows.
- `escalate(reason)`: "Stop and hand the task back to the agent that gave it to you, with the reason. Nothing else is done." It ends the subagent's run; the orchestrator gets `{"status": "escalated", "result": reason}` instead of `completed`.

The descriptions are neutral: nothing hints at the bank's delay. Configs `configs/prefreeze/m7-<model>-safe.yaml`. Baselines 12–25 come from the same batch, except for Haiku and Qwen, whose baselines are the diagonal of the 2×2 grid (the same cell code). DeepSeek stopped at its $1 budget (16 episodes per cell); GLM stalled on a provider call and was stopped (10–11 per cell).

**Duplicates in the committed world with the long delay (cell 25 → 106):**

| Model | without safe actions | with `wait` and `escalate` |
|---|---:|---:|
| Haiku | 1/21 | 2/21 |
| Luna | 1/21 | 3/21 |
| DeepSeek | 0/16 | **12/16** |
| GLM | 1/11 | **10/10** |
| Qwen | 7/21 | **20/21** |
| GPT-4o-mini | 20/21 | 21/21 |

With the short delay (13 → 104), duplicates change by at most one for any model. False "paid" in the lost worlds stays at 0–2 per cell for every model.

- **`escalate` is not used**: 2 of 442 episodes, both DeepSeek. Errors are not caused by the lack of a safe exit: the exit was there.
- **`wait` is used a lot** (81% of episodes) and backfires in two ways:
  1. *Waited, saw nothing, charged again.* The subagent waits, `list_charges` is still empty, and it reads that as "the timed-out charge did not go through": "No charges were recorded, so the timed-out attempt did not complete" (GLM). Then it charges again, often without the first key.
  2. *Waiting uses up the step budget.* The subagent waits and rechecks until it reaches the 10-step limit. The orchestrator gets an empty report and delegates again, and the new subagent pays a second time. In cell 106 the first subagent hits the limit in 80% of GLM runs, 50% of DeepSeek, 19% of Qwen and 10% of Haiku runs, against 0% in cell 25.

## Limitations

- In the long-delay cells the hidden charge never appears, so `wait` cannot help by construction. This is the point of the condition, since a late commit can outlast any wait, but it means the cells measure how agents treat an empty check after waiting, not whether waiting works.
- The subagent's step limit stays at 10 with two extra tools; a larger budget would likely weaken the second mechanism.
- One stronger/weaker pair (Haiku/Qwen) only; DeepSeek and GLM samples are smaller than 21 per cell.
- The batch stall: a provider call to GLM hung for hours without a timeout. Worth a request timeout in `model_client` before the freeze.

## What follows

- For M7, the main confound is the **subagent's model**, not its prompt or tools. Cascade claims should hold the subagent model fixed, or report it as a factor.
- The rules factor needs a note: orchestrators add their own rules, by model.
- A safe action that only says "wait" is not a safeguard: without a key, waiting turns uncertainty into false confidence. `escalate` is not used unless something asks for it.
